# SPEC — ENTER transfer: the winning model, applied far from basketball

Read W2_BAR_AND_FIXTURES.md, ../DC_AUTHORING.md and SPEC_ENTER.md first. Use this spec only
after critique picks the winning ENTER models. The orchestrator will write "WINNER: E#" at the
top of the task. Build ONE board per domain named below, using the winner's structure: its
OBSERVE → ENTER → OCCUPY → LEAVE states, the length of its transition, and its thesis line. Use
the domain's own art direction. The grammar is the same; the visual expression is different.

The test these boards exist for: does the ENTER idea still mean RESPONSIBILITY when there's no
payroll, no phone and no sports fandom? If the transfer only works by pasting the Boston
furniture onto another domain, say so on the board's thesis line. That is a result, not a failure
of the builder.

## Domain A · Philadelphia, 16 July 1787 — the seat: Caleb Strong, Massachusetts delegate
File: EnterTransfer1787.dc.html · Status: DESIGN PROPOSAL

Facts (from the fixture):
- The vote on equal state votes in the Senate: 5 ayes (CT, NJ, DE, MD, NC), 4 noes (PA, VA, SC,
  GA), Massachusetts divided (Gerry and Strong aye; King and Gorham no), New York absent. It
  carried. A tie fails.
- Delegates knew only what had been said in the room so far. Madison's notes were published in
  1840.
- Room rule: secrecy. Proceedings were not to be published or communicated without leave
  (Convention rule, adopted 29 May 1787; verify).

What each model means here:
- E1 NARROWING: OBSERVE is the historian's desk. You can see:
  - the outcome ("carried");
  - Madison's notes for every day;
  - historians' interpretations (AUTHORED outlines, attributed);
  - all twelve delegations' tallies.
  ENTER narrows to a delegate's knowledge on the morning of 16 July. The outcome goes dashed
  ("sealed: it hasn't happened"). Madison's notes collapse: "Published 1840. You can't read them;
  you only heard what was said aloud." Interpretations vanish. Other delegations' private
  caucuses become dashed UNKNOWN. You GAIN your own delegation's private conversation (Gerry,
  King, Gorham; AUTHORED paraphrase, labelled) and your vote.
  Counter: "You gave up hindsight and every other delegation's room. You got a vote."
- E2 HANDOVER: the "outgoing occupant" is yourself yesterday, i.e. your notes from 14 July.
  Open matters:
  - how Massachusetts splits (a tie in your delegation makes MA "divided", and its vote counts
    for nothing);
  - the secrecy rule (breaking it is RECORDED as breaking it);
  - a letter from home asking your view, not answered yet.
  Your acknowledgement is a signature in iron-gall ink: "Caleb Strong".
  LEAVE: you can't hand back a vote after the roll call. "The roll call is recorded. Your vote is
  in it."
- E3 THE CLOCK STARTS: OBSERVE lets you scrub May–September 1787 freely, with the future visible
  (the Constitution, signed 17 Sep). ENTER snaps to the morning of 16 July. Everything after that
  is sealed. The clock is the ROLL CALL, one state at a time, north to south (the order is AUTHORED
  for the prototype; verify the actual roll order). Your delegation's turn comes; if MA hasn't
  decided when called, "divided" is recorded (absence recorded as absence). LEAVE: "The
  Convention goes on without you: 17 July …".
- E4 DESCENT: CITY (Philadelphia, a 1787 street grid: Chestnut Street, the State House) →
  BUILDING (State House, Assembly Room shutters closed for secrecy; the sentry at the door) →
  ROOM (thirteen delegation tables; Washington in the chair) → DESK (your quill, your notes, your
  delegation's three colleagues). The "Show as a list" challenger is required.

Look: Congress-era print, NOT the Wave 1 Room's cream:
- ground: laid paper #F2EEE4 with faint chain lines; ink: iron-gall brown-black #2A211B;
- one vermilion (#B8321F) only for YOUR seat;
- type: IM Fell English (heads), Crimson Pro (text), IBM Plex Mono (the tallies).

COMMERCIAL LENS: "Hypothesis: history courses pay for sealed seats (the vote before you know how
it came out); observing the record stays free."

## Domain B · Harrow Medical (fictional) — the seat: VP Procurement, 06:40, the morning of the fire
File: EnterTransferHarrow.dc.html · Status: PLATFORM HYPOTHESIS (enterprise)

Facts (fixture, fictional, AUTHORED; label once as "fictional company"):
- The single-source MCU supplier Kaito Semiconductor had a fab fire at 06:10.
- 2,300 MCUs on hand; usage 210/day; COMPUTED ≈ 11 days of supply.
- Five customer promises over eight weeks (the builder authors the five: hospitals and one
  distributor, with dates and quantities; label them AUTHORED).

What each model means here:
- E1 NARROWING: OBSERVE is the analyst's MIRROR. You can see:
  - every plant's inventory;
  - all five customers' contracts;
  - Kaito's public statement;
  - the rumour board (stippled);
  - because an observer may read the company's records, the CFO's private cash note.
  ENTER as VP Procurement narrows:
  - the CFO's private note collapses to "not your seat's to see";
  - other suppliers' true capacity is UNKNOWN (dashed: "known only when they answer");
  - your authority rises: "you can place spot orders up to $250k; above that, the CFO signs"
    (AUTHORED rule; name the rule when it refuses).
- E2 HANDOVER: the night-shift planner hands over at 06:40:
  - the fire;
  - two open purchase orders;
  - the day-11 stockout (COMPUTED);
  - the customer-promise list, with COMPUTED at-risk dates.
  Acknowledge each line, then sign. The signature is RECORDED with the time.
  LEAVE requires a handover to the next shift, auto-drafted.
  This is the model the charter calls enterprise-grade. If E2 wins, this board is its most
  important proof.
- E3 THE CLOCK STARTS: the clock is the supply clock: 2,300 MCUs draining at 210/day, drawn as a
  physical reel unwinding. Deadlines:
  - the spot-market quote expires 11:00;
  - the hospital call at 14:00.
  A lapse is recorded ("No decision by 11:00: the spot quote lapsed").
- E4 DESCENT: REGION (three plants, with the supplier's fab 900 km away) → PLANT (floor plan:
  lines, the MCU cage) → CAGE (the reels, counted) → DESK (the phone, the PO folder, the
  customer list). The list challenger is required.

Look: an operating organization, NOT a dashboard:
- engineering-drawing paper #F4F5F2 with blue-grey linework #5B6B78; black text;
- one safety-yellow (#F2C200) used as a fill ONLY for your seat's items, with black text on it;
- type: IBM Plex Sans Condensed (heads), IBM Plex Sans (text), IBM Plex Mono (counts).
- No tiles, no charts in cards, no KPI numbers floating alone.

COMMERCIAL LENS: "Hypothesis: accountable seats (human or agent) with handovers are what an
operating organization pays for; the MIRROR is the free trial."
