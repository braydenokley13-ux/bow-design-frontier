## 2. ENTER canon
One protocol, several carriers: **grant → redeem → lease.** E0 Handover is the core (you take named
matters; a blank line is refused because it would be a choice nobody made; lapses are recorded as NO
ACT; leaving is a handover or vacate). E3's rule: authority arrives as an Offer redeemed by a present
person in chrome only BOW draws. E2's rule: hindsight is a stamp that rides every act and fork. E4 Ring
is the second mode of entry (the seat calls you; picking up is entering for the call; standing orders
answer while you're away). E5 governs multi-party acts (a seat is the unheld face of a shared paper).
E1 folds into the Room. E6 is killed as ENTER and kept as the Pull instrument. The visible sign of
having crossed: Jurisdiction's standing line ("your word counts for … · not for …") plus Guest's
five-part threshold (who offers · which seat · what you'll see · what you give up · what stays private).

## 3. One vocabulary for the seam
Reality grading a branch uses exactly four verdicts, everywhere: **HELD · DIVERGED · IMPOSSIBLE ·
RE-DECLARE**. (Retire torn / pulled / stitched / open / "breaks here".)

## 4. Record and address laws (from both critics)
1. **Two logs, never merged.** The canonical RECORD (acts that change state; append-only; numbered once
   per World; hashed) and the person's TRAIL (navigation, toggles, reveals; private; never hashed).
2. **SHA-256** over the canonical record only (never a 24- or 32-bit toy hash; never over navigation).
3. **One World, one numbering, one cash.** The shared fixture now carries canonical act numbers and
   World cash; boards read them, never restate them.
4. **The address lives in the URL** (`location.hash`) and reloading restores the state it names. An
   address names STATE, never grants authority: a `seat` segment is a VIEWPOINT — opening it yields
   exactly what an observer may see, and says so. Authority travels only as an Offer.
5. **Kind propagation:** a result wears the kind of its weakest input. An authored stand-in feeding a
   computed rule yields AUTHORED output, not COMPUTED. MODELED only when a named, versioned model ran
   (a value copied from a model's output elsewhere says where it ran and is verify).
6. **Truth textures must not read as form controls.** No hollow squares next to text. Use texture on
   the value itself (underline, fill, rule) or swatches shaped unlike checkboxes (bars, lozenges).
7. **Never silently replace user input.** A typed ask that the prototype cannot resolve becomes an
   honest "no system for this" or a Want filed under the place it names.
8. **Viewports:** 1440×900, 1280×800, 1024×600 and 390×844 (a phone gets a phone layout, not a picture
   of a phone).

## 5. Strongest two for the cross-domain attack
**Jurisdiction** (the act grammar: forms whose lines other systems hold; kinds of yes; standing line;
registers) and **Cut** (the time grammar: four planes, the seal, the rail, the seam). Guest is the
strangest-important and is carried into the convergence proof, not the cross-domain attack.

## 6. The convergence proof (the ONE flow)
Both critics named the same flow: the seat rings you → you pick up and your standing line rewrites →
you sign your line of the Denver paper → the League returns it naming the rule → you fix and resend →
act lands in the Room, the Table and Direct at once with one act number and one SHA-256 → you leave;
a standing order answers while you're away → the Round on return says what you can no longer do and
which of your kept assumptions Reality broke (HELD / DIVERGED / IMPOSSIBLE / RE-DECLARE) → you lift the
trade out to see what it did → you keep the fork → return to the record. On ONE record, addressed in
the URL, checked across two clients. Built as `prototypes/OneRecord.html`.
