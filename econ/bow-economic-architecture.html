BOW Economic Architecture

Sixteen places money could plausibly enter an executable-world medium, what each one costs to serve, and what that means for design. This is an input to pricing work. It commits BOW to nothing and contains no prices.

STATUS Everything below is a hypothesis for founder review. No market sizes, prices or revenue figures are stated, on purpose. “Free” and “open” recommendations are design proposals, not policy.

The distribution question

What must be free for the medium to spread

A shared Moment that asks for payment before the door is a dead link. Executable reality travels the way video did: the object moves freely, and the business sits behind persistence, depth, data and scale. The line between free and paid should fall after the door, never before it.

Free, and designed to travel

  • Entering any shared Moment, with one decision, without an account.
  • Opening a public system as an observer, and following it.
  • Reading provenance. “How do we know?” is never behind a paywall.
  • Forking a Moment once, and sharing that branch.
  • The World format, truth tags and renderer contract, so others can build and render.
  • A creator tier that can publish small Moments.
  • A teacher trial with a real lesson, not a demo.

Where money plausibly lives

  • Persistent Worlds that keep running while you are away.
  • Deep Lab runs and model execution, metered where they happen.
  • Licensed live Reality data.
  • Premium and official Worlds and institutions.
  • Professional and enterprise seats with warrants and receipts.
  • Creator, studio and publisher plans; marketplace commerce.
  • Runtime for companies building their own Worlds.

Design implications

What the economics asks of the design

The door is always free

Every Moment needs a free first decision and one honest line showing where paid begins. The paywall sits after the door and names what it pays for: persistence, depth or data.

Persistence is the premium feeling

“Your World kept running while you were away” is the paid experience. Design the return: what changed, what waited for you, what you missed.

Show compute where it is spent

A Lab run or a model call should carry a visible cost line, like the provenance line beside it. A meter is part of telling the truth about a system.

Bring your own intelligence

A provider setting (BOW-managed, your own key, an enterprise gateway, a local model) is a first-class control. Price the orchestration and the seat, not the tokens.

Licences live in the provenance

A licensed live value should know its licence the way it knows its source. The same tag that says OBSERVED can say who may see it, and for how long.

Attribution travels with the object

Remix and fork chains carry creator credit inside the Moment itself, so marketplace revenue shares can follow the object wherever it goes.

Student data stays inside the evidence boundary

Schools and districts buy trust. No advertising model, no student data in public forks, and a clear line between a child’s run and anything shared.

Receipts are the enterprise product

Warrants, receipts and escalations are what an enterprise audits and pays for. Design them to hold up as evidence, not as an activity feed.

Never charge for the format

Network and Cloud revenue depend on many parties adopting one open format. Charge for hosting, running and connecting Worlds, never for describing one.

For the pricing work that follows

Open questions

  1. Is the paid unit a World, a seat inside a World, or time a World spends running? Each implies a different product.
  2. Who pays for Reality data when a free Moment shows a live value: the publisher, the viewer, or BOW as a distribution cost?
  3. Should model execution be pass-through with a margin, bundled into seats, or free up to a quota with bring-your-own-provider beyond it?
  4. How much of Foundry can be free before authoring cost (review, compilation) outruns creator revenue?
  5. Do team and league rights holders become buyers, sellers or partners? The answer changes the whole sports surface. Escalate before assuming.
  6. What is the cheapest commercial falsifier for each surface, run before building it? The Commercial Lens board on the design canvas proposes one per frontier.
