Wave 1 proved BOW can be designed. It found eight behaviours that only make sense in a medium of executable systems — and a grammar that drifted as soon as eight builders used it.

Survived
  • Sealed hindsight — the future is hidden until you choose, and what you knew is kept with the choice (Fork).
  • Declared others — a branch asks you to declare what other actors do instead of pretending to compute it (Fork).
  • Unknowns split, never averaged — an unknowable event becomes two labelled ranges (Fork).
  • A value whose texture changes as the system settles — modeled on its way, computed at rest (Chemistry).
  • The World-versus-Reality reading of WHY — the same causal links are on the record in a World and estimates in reality (Lens).
  • Refusal that names its rule — the warrant refuses, not the model’s mood (Agent, Foundry).
  • The knowledge horizon — a seat states what it knows, can do, can’t do, and what nobody in it could know (Seat).
  • The address as a readable coordinate — system · moment · branch · role · view (Sentence, Fork, Sports).
Died or parked
  • Chemistry v1’s five-panel dashboard — killed; rebuilt as one instrument with a lens projected from the barrel.
  • Fork v1’s “modeled” win bands — killed; they were a designer’s guess wearing a model’s texture.
  • Hatch as a branch fill — killed across boards; a branch is a frame, an ink and a stamp.
  • Network as a standalone ledger — parked; a filterable table, not an interaction. Replaced by the Wave 2 Handshake.
  • The Arithmetic as its own board — to be absorbed into the Room as Strong’s tally sheet.
  • The Seat and the Focus as landing pages — folded in: the Seat’s horizon becomes the River’s threshold; the Focus’s ladder joins Biology’s spine as the scale lens.
  • “The Desk” Browser challenger — killed before building; the founder had already rejected the tan desk and papers.
Unresolved
  • Is ENTER a transition of responsibility or just a nicer button? Most boards let you look; few make you answerable.
  • What does a fork owe you after it’s cut — a guess, a range, or only what can be computed?
  • Can one WHAT IF? gesture carry a history branch, a lab state and a governance change?
  • Can the seven textures stay legible to a 14-year-old without the words?
  • What discretion should an occupant — person or model — have inside a warrant?

The Browser, after Wave 1: four challengers became one front door

Critics’ synthesis · a proposal, not canon
1 · The door is a momentThe RiverA system’s life, with doors at moments you can enter. Each door is named by the question people faced, never by what happened next.
→
2 · The threshold is a seatFrom the SeatChoose who you are at that moment and read the chair’s horizon: know · can · can’t · nobody in it knows. Real people are observed, not worn.
→
3 · Arrival is a choiceThe first real decisionThe morning of — with the future sealed. What you do is written to your branch, never to the record.
Across all three: the address (from the Sentence). One editable line every door writes into — system · moment · seat · branch · view — shareable, and typed directly by people who already know where they’re going.
Inside any arrival: the scale lens (Biology’s spine, with the Focus’s ladder). Wider / finer, carrying one state — change a salary at the contract and Boston’s mark crosses a line at the league. For a process with no “morning of”, scale is the door and time becomes the how-do-we-know lens.

What Wave 1 actually taught

  1. The grammar transfers; the skin doesn’t have to. Seventeen boards in fifteen visual languages used the same seven textures and the same fork stamp. The founder’s rule — shared grammar, different expression — held.
  2. The grammar drifted the moment many hands used it. Hatch came to mean model, interpretation and branch; solid meant fact and opinion. Five rules now bind every builder (in the Wave 2 brief).
  3. The most common truth failure was ours: numbers the designer wrote wearing a model’s texture. Rule: authored until a model actually runs.
  4. Several boards fell back into known categories: the Arithmetic is an interactive chart, Network a filterable table, Enterprise nearly a Gantt chart, Foundry a validation wizard. Where they escaped, it was because an act changed state that the person then had to live with.
  5. Role is weak almost everywhere. Boards let you look; few make you answerable. That is why ENTER gets its own tournament in Wave 2.
  6. Sports carried the medium best — real stakes, a real record to compare against, and pull. The APUSH boards produced the richest drawings, but APUSH is a stress test, not the center (founder’s correction, accepted).
  7. The critique loop works when critics see renders. Every serious finding came from screenshots: a seal that didn’t seal, a hindsight leak in a “consensus” board, a hatch that buried the record, a trade detail that was simply wrong.

Dissent, preserved

  • Fork: the critic would show only computed ledgers after a fork. I kept labelled guess ranges for three seasons because people will ask “would we have won?” Unresolved until people use it.
  • Chemistry: removing the “state strip” made the instrument cleaner but hides the idea that there is one canonical state. Both readings are defensible.
  • Arithmetic: the critic says absorb it into the Room. The counter-case: a mechanism view is the only board that makes institutional economics computable. Kept alive as a component.
  • Agent: the critic warns that identical behaviour across occupants teaches “models are interchangeable.” The counter-case: the warrant, not the occupant, is the point. Open question, flagged on the board.
  • Browser: the first critic saw the Sentence as the shared entry layer; the second says it is the address, not a door, and that time is the default. For processes (respiration) the second critic itself concedes scale is the door. Held open for the founder.
  • Lens: one-cause-per-sheet is narrower than reality. The critic wants two or three causes per sheet with the user’s pick marked as authored. Worth building.

Decisions only the founder can make

  1. Is the address — system · moment · branch · role · view — the core object of the medium?
  2. Truth grammar: texture-first as proposed, and unify the three dialects already in the repos (real/derived/modeled/fiction; FACT/YOUR BOOKS/FORECAST/SIGNAL/RUMOR; then/act/model/later/never)?
  3. Runtime actor models and agents cross the Decision Challenges line “no LLM-authored money, terms, obligations or consequences.” Allowed outside assessment, inside a warrant?
  4. The ethics of role: adopt “observe with sources” seats as a principle for history?
  5. Real people and teams in Worlds: team marks, player names and likeness need a rights posture before anything ships. Escalate, don’t assume.
  6. Is time the default front door of the Browser (River → seat → arrival), with the address across it and scale as a lens?
  7. May the Lab show win probabilities for real teams and players, even labelled as modeled?

How this was judged

Every board was rendered in a local Chromium harness running the canvas’s own runtime and played through its interaction path; screenshots of each state went to independent critics who had not built the boards. Builders never judged their own work. Critics checked facts where they could and found real errors (the 2025 Porziņģis trade return, the June 2017 timeline, a hindsight leak in a “consensus” draft board). Nothing here has met a student, fan, teacher or operator; no verdict below is evidence of anything but a design reading.

Wave 2 (next page onward) takes the founder’s update: Browser as the flagship shell, sports as the proving ground, ENTER and WHAT IF? tournaments, Search/Atlas, Publisher, Cloud, a real Network interaction, and a commercial lens on everything.
