# CLOSED ADVOCATE: own the World, publish the receipts

**Position.** BOW should stay vertically integrated and proprietary: BOW writes, sequences, verifies and curates; anyone may read, cite and check. "Closed" means closed *authority*, not hidden *logic*. "Foreseeable future" ends when the tripwires in section 9 fire. I argue to win and mark speculation.

## 1. The clone test is an argument for closed

Facts (CloneVerdict): four engines copied from seven screenshots in 12-22 calls each. The copies failed on what no screenshot prints: an unprinted $205.0M apron line, derived-secret leaks, the exact hash scheme. The copy "can never be this World" and could not verify against our record.

I concede the first half. Screens, grammar and engine code are not a moat; secrecy never was. Wave 1's own audit says the real moat (a canonical World, a verified rulebook, accumulated history) is **zero today**. A closed strategy defends nothing now. Its job is to buy the 12-24 months in which those three assets get built.

The second half is the point. **The only friction the clone test measured is the unprinted part, and a public write-spec prints it.** Publish the schema, hash scheme, taint rules and status semantics, and the copier's miss list goes to zero. "Cannot verify against the original" becomes "verifies fine." That does more than leak code: it manufactures a *compatible rival* that mints records shaped like ours. If a chatbot rebuilds any screen in a day, the scarce thing is authority over what counts as canonical, and authority is an operator, not a spec. (Speculation: copy speed keeps falling.)

## 2. Trust and security: the hard properties need an authority

BOW's differentiators (seat-scoped knowledge, hidden bids, real deadlines, a Commissioner clock, private student runs) all need a party that can **withhold, sequence and time-stamp**. In a federated open runtime, hosts distrust each other, and a third-party host holding a seat's private facts can leak them. Nothing built so far handles that: Live World's authority is a host platform's ACL ("no cryptographic signature by BOW"), and even on one trusted host the critics found existence and timing leaks. Standardizing a multi-host protocol before we can close leaks on one host is the wrong problem.

Trust in a closed medium:
1. **Central verification.** BOW signs record heads (full 64-hex, CloneVerdict build C). Verification stays read-open: anyone can replay a receipt under its pinned model. Only BOW-signed heads are canonical.
2. **A rulebook registry** with dated sources and a regression corpus of real trades, ruled on both sides.
3. **Statuses emitted by the engine from provenance**, never hand-tagged (avoids Semantic-Web "Metacrap").
4. **Review before publish** for third-party content.

For children this is also compliance. FERPA/COPPA want one accountable operator, and Decision Challenges' own law (permanent competency ids, no pooling across versions, attributable human review) holds only if one party controls who can emit "assessment" records. An open writer ecosystem invites credential inflation. Nintendo's seal and lockout chip (1985, after the 1983 crash of an unlicensed Atari ecosystem) and App Store review made the same trade: central verification is what made third-party supply trustworthy. (Crash causation is debated.)

Rights point (speculation): a league or data licensor will license live feeds to an accountable operator, not into a format anyone can write.

## 3. Adoption, monetization, ecosystem, network effects

**Adoption.** The bottleneck is zero classroom users, not third-party adopters. Growth travels through the free door (enter a Moment, observe, read provenance, fork once), a distribution policy compatible with a closed format. I accept "never charge for the format": reading is free, and we publish no writer spec. Closed also lets the wire format change weekly. Today there are five `bow://` grammars and three non-identical canonical-JSON functions. Every published version is a forever replay obligation (Doom demos desync; Minecraft seeds changed at 1.18; Excel still carries Lotus's 1900 leap-year bug).

**Monetization** sits at L3 (persistent hosting and time), L6 (licensed data), L8 (take rate), L9 (institutional licences and receipts). All require being the authority. An open format makes L3 a commodity others host, which is what happened to open cores: MongoDB (SSPL, 2018), Elastic (2021; AWS forked OpenSearch), HashiCorp (BSL, 2023; OpenTofu fork).

**Ecosystem.** Controlled ecosystems recruit when they bring audience and payment. Roblox keeps a closed engine and paid creators $741M in 2023 and $923M in 2024. iOS ran web-apps-only until the SDK (March 2008), then App Store review. Unreal is source-*available* with a 5% royalty. Figma shipped a read API in 2018, plugins (write) in spring 2019, and still has a proprietary format. Minecraft's closed engine thrived on community mods even though Mojang's promised official API never shipped.

**Network effects.** Ours are *referee-dependent*: a shared canonical World, cross-World contracts (`bow-bridge-1`), a history that actually happened. An open standard's network effects accrue to its users, not its originator.

## 4. Analogies, including counter-cases

- **Won closed:** Bloomberg Terminal (one vendor; it opened *identifiers*, FIGI, only in 2015 once it owned the market). Palantir's Ontology is the nearest "executable systems" analogue, sold whole. Excel dominated on a closed binary format whose specification came out in 2008. Apple gave QuickTime's container to MPEG after it had won authoring.
- **Lost or stalled:** Flash (a plugin needing a gatekeeper's permission), HyperCard (one runtime, one sponsor), Xanadu (specified everything, shipped nothing), VisiCalc (no lock; out-built by Lotus 1-2-3). Second Life open-sourced its viewer in January 2007 while the grid stayed closed, and OpenSim reimplemented the server. Growth stalled on onboarding, so it is a mixed case for both sides.
- **Open won, honestly:** the web, PDF, MP4, git, LSP. Each is a waist between many *already-existing* independent parties. BOW has one party.

## 5. Failure modes of my own strategy

1. **Xanadu risk:** closed and never used. Mitigation: one vertical, one class, five fans, no spec.
2. **Hostage risk:** Flash and HyperCard users lost their work. Mitigation: static exports (PDF, video, CSV) of any record, plus written **escrow**: the replay spec for any record version is published on sunset or change of control.
3. **Ceiling:** institutions demand exit and interop. Mitigation: *consume* others' standards at the edge (LTI, OneRoster, SSO) and invent none.
4. **Open rival with distribution** standardizes first (Android's share over iOS; Lotus over VisiCalc). Then our record is an island. I cannot rule this out.
5. **Liability:** the operator owns child safety (Roblox faces litigation) and, at scale, gatekeeper regulation (Epic v. Apple, EU DMA).
6. **Cost:** a small team owning the whole stack widens slowly.

## 6. Per-layer call

| Layer | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| L0 record | **Closed to write; read-open verifier** | Receipts verify anywhere; no outside writers until a leak model exists |
| L1 address | Open to cite; **spec not yet** | Five grammars; unify internally first |
| L2 rulebook / contract | **Closed contract; public rule text and sources** | Transparency stays; standardizing execution semantics now is VRML |
| L3 runtime / authority | **Closed, operator-owned** | The crown: time, seats, identity, secrets |
| L4 representation | First-party; exports open; packs **not yet** | Views derive from the record |
| L5 network | First-party bilateral only | `bow-bridge-1` is unverified rehearsal |
| L6 connectors | Closed, licensed | Licences bind to one operator |
| L7 intelligence | Closed harness; models interchangeable behind it | Occupant acts are runtime-signed records |
| L8 creators | Curated Foundry, review, rev share; marketplace **not yet** | Review cost unknown |
| L9 verticals | Education first-party; enterprise a separate later call | |

## 7. Next 12 months

1. **0-3 mo:** Both products into real classrooms, one non-BOW teacher running them unaided. Merge the five grammars and three serializers into one *internal* versioned record spec with an archival replay corpus.
2. **3-6 mo:** Signed heads, full hashes. The 10-15 dated, sourced, both-sides trade corpus. Public receipt verifier and static exports.
3. **6-9 mo:** Invite 3-5 creators into a review-gated Foundry. Write the escrow policy.
4. **9-12 mo:** Read the tripwires. No SDK, marketplace or federation before then.

## 8. Strongest objection

"A medium is open by definition. Neutral formats (web, PDF, MP4) win, and one vendor's record dies like Flash." **Answer:** neutrality solves multiparty distrust, and BOW has one party. Most cited open media were standardized *after* dominance: PDF (ISO 2008), MP4 (2003), Excel's binary format (2008). The web is the exception. The decisive point is **optionality asymmetry**: opening later is cheap and welcomed; closing later is costly and forks the community (OpenSearch, OpenTofu; Elastic and Redis later added AGPL to win trust back). Closed keeps the irreversible option unspent. Where the objection still bites (institutions wanting exit), exports and escrow answer it. The concession: if a rival opens first with distribution, we lose the medium race and keep the education business.

## 9. Tripwires to open

Publish a read/replay spec when **two** hold: (a) a non-BOW author ships a World unaided and a stranger forks it; (b) a paying institution requires a second reader; (c) a rival open format shows real adopters. Otherwise stay closed.
